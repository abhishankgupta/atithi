India is welcoming and most trips go smoothly. Knowing a few local systems makes it easier still. Save these numbers before you land.

## Emergency numbers

- **112** — all emergencies: police, fire and ambulance.
- **1363** or **1800-11-1363** — the Ministry of Tourism's 24×7 tourist helpline, in 12 languages.

## Before you go

- Apply for an e-Visa only on the official government website. Look-alike sites charge extra fees.
- Get travel insurance that covers medical evacuation, especially for Ladakh or treks.
- See a travel clinic 4–6 weeks before you fly about vaccines.
- Keep paper and digital copies of your passport and visa.

## Getting around

- Use airport prepaid taxi counters or app cabs; avoid unmarked cars.
- Book trains through the official railway app or your agent.
- Share your live location with someone when travelling at night.
- Delhi Metro has women-only carriages at the front of each train.

## Common scams

- "Your hotel (or the monument) is closed" — always check yourself.
- Gem or carpet "export deals" that promise profit at home.
- Unofficial guides at monuments: use licensed guides with ID cards.
- Agree taxi and rickshaw fares before the ride starts.

## Women travellers

- Dressing modestly in small towns and at religious sites reduces unwanted attention.
- Choose well-reviewed stays, and ask your agent for a female guide or driver if you prefer.
- Trust your instincts and leave any situation that feels wrong.

## Health

- Drink sealed or filtered water, and carry oral rehydration salts.
- Use mosquito repellent, especially at dusk in the monsoon months.
- Private hospitals in major cities offer high-quality care.
- In April to June, protect yourself from heat: shade, a hat and electrolytes.

## Respect and etiquette

- Remove your shoes before entering temples and homes.
- Eat and pass things with your right hand.
- Ask before photographing people, especially women and religious rituals.
