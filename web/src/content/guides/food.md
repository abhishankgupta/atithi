"Indian food" is really dozens of cuisines. Here's what to look for where — and how to enjoy it without upsetting your stomach.

## Region by region

- **North:** butter chicken, dal makhani, chole bhature, rogan josh from Kashmir, and kulfi.
- **South:** masala dosa, idli, Chettinad chicken, Hyderabadi biryani and filter coffee.
- **West:** Mumbai's vada pav and pav bhaji, a Gujarati thali, and Goan fish curry.
- **East and Northeast:** Kolkata kathi rolls, Bengali fish curry, rasgulla, momos and smoked pork.

## Street-food rules that work

1. Eat where locals queue — high turnover means fresh food.
2. Choose dishes cooked hot in front of you: dosa, paratha, pakora, tikka.
3. For the first few days, skip raw salads, cut fruit, ice and chutneys left uncovered.
4. Drink sealed bottled water or filtered water from your hotel.
5. Ask for food "less spicy", and carry oral rehydration salts just in case.

## Dietary needs

India is one of the easiest countries in the world for vegetarians. Vegan, Jain (no root vegetables), halal and gluten-free food are widely understood in cities — tell your agent and they can brief restaurants and hotels.

## Handy phrases

- **Kam teekha** — less spicy
- **Bina mirch** — no chilli
- **Shakahari** — vegetarian
- **Kitna hua?** — how much?
- **Dhanyavaad** — thank you
